# FUTURE_DS_02
Telco Customer Churn Analysis — Analyzing customer behavior to identify churn drivers, high-risk customer segments, tenure patterns, and actionable strategies for improving customer retention using Excel and Power BI.


1. Data Preparation and Cleaning

Before Conducting the analysis, several data preparation activities were performed to ensure the dataset was accurate, reliable, and suitable for analysis. 

The first step involved reviewing the dataset to gain an understanding of it's structure, variables, and overall content. This process helped identify important field such as Customer demographics, tenure, contract type, payment method, internet service, monthly charges, and churn status that would later support the analysis.

Therefore, the dataset was cleaned and organsed using Microsoft Excel. Duplicate record were identified and removed to avoid inaccurate results. Additionally each column was examined for missing values, and where necessary, these vvalue were appropriately addressed to maintain data quality and consistency. 

Completing these preparation processes was important in ensuring that the dataset was complete, accurate, and ready for meaningful business analysis.


2. Why are Customers leaving the platform

The analysis indicates that customers are leaving primarily due to contract type payment method, and lack of support service. Customers on month-to-month contracts show a significantly higher churn rate of 42.7% compared to one-year and two-year contracts. This shows that customers on short-term commitment are more likely to leave.

Additionally, customers using electronic check as a payment methods have the highest churn rate of 45.2% compared to other payment methods. Furthermore, absence of tech support and online service which has higher monthly charges, are also contributing factors to customer churn. 


3. Which Customer Segments are most likely to churn

The analysis revealed that high risk segments include month-to-month customers, customers using electronic check payments, and customers with fiber optic internet service without tech support. These groups show the highest likelihood of churning compared to other segments 

In addition, customers in the early stage of their lifecycle, particularly within the first 18 months, are more vulnerable to churn. Senior citizen and customers without partners or dependents also show a higher tendency to churn. These segments should be priotized for targeted retention strategies.


4. How long do customers typically stay active

Customer tenure provide an important indication of loyalty and length of stay. The overall average tenure is approxinately 32 months, which represents the typical length of time customers remain active on the platflorm.

However, there is a clear difference between retained and churned customers. Customers who remain active have an average tenure of 37.57 months, while customers who churned have an average tenure of only 17.98 months. This indicates that most churn occurs early,and the 18 months are the most critical period for retention. 


5. What Actions can improve customer retention

To improve customer retention, the business should focus on converting month-to-month customers to long-term contracts by offering discounts and incentices. This will help reduce the high churn associated with short-term contracts.

The business should also enhance the customer support experience by providing technical support and online security, especially for fiber optic customers. It should review and improve the electronic check payment experience, as this group has the highest churn rate. Additionally, implementing an onboarding and engagement program during the first 18 months will help retain new customers. Continuous monitoring of high-risk segments through Power BI dashboard will allow for proactive intervention before churn occures. 


6. conclusion

This dataset shows that churn is mainly driven by month-to-month contracts(42.7%) and electronic check payments(45.2).Churned customers stay only 17.98 months on average, compared to 37.57 months for retained customers, showing the first 18 months are critical.

By moving customers to long-term contracts, improving tech support, and echancing the payment experience, the business can reduce churn, keep customers longer and grow faster.

7. Recommendations

Encourage long-term contracts. Offer discounts and incentives to move month to month customer to 1-year and 2-year contracts to reduce 42.7%churn.
Improve electronic check experience. Fix payment issues and promote auto-pay options like bank transfer and credit card, as electronic check has 45.2% churn.
Enhance support services. Provide free tech support and online security for fiber optic customers to improve satisfaction.
